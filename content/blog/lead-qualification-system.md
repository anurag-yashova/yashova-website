Every unqualified lead costs the same to acquire and far more to service. The fix is not better targeting. It is sorting the list before anyone picks up the phone.

## Qualify in the form, not on the call

The instinct is to keep forms short and qualify during the sales call. That protects lead volume and destroys sales capacity — your team spends its best hours discovering that someone was browsing.

Move the sorting upstream. Yes, fewer people complete the form. That is the mechanism working.

## The four questions that do most of the work

**1. Timeline.** "How soon do you want to start?" with options like *right away*, *this week*, *this month*, *just exploring*. This single question is the most useful sorting signal you will get, and it is self-reported honestly because it feels harmless.

**2. Fit.** Something binary that determines whether you can actually serve them — background, category, location, business type. On an education funnel this was "are you from a science background?"

**3. Budget or scale.** A range, never an exact figure. People answer ranges and abandon forms over exact numbers.

**4. Preference.** Language, channel, or time to contact. Cheap to answer, and it makes the first human contact land better.

## What to do with each segment

**Right away:** contact within minutes. Full sales attention.

**This week:** contact same day. Standard sequence.

**This month:** automated nurture with a human touch at the right moment. Do not burn sales hours here yet.

**Just exploring:** long-term nurture. No sales calls. Most will never buy; a few will, months later, and automation is what keeps you present until then.

Treating all four identically is how teams stay busy and close little.

## Scoring beyond the form

Behaviour after submission tells you more than the form did:

- Replied to the WhatsApp confirmation
- Attended the webinar or demo
- Opened the pricing page
- Asked a specific question

An "exploring" lead who attends the full session and asks about fees has re-scored themselves. Your system should notice that.

## The number that proves it works

On a medical coding funnel, 15,000 raw leads passed through four form questions and became 5,800 qualified. The sales team worked those 5,800 and closed 780.

Had they worked all 15,000, each lead would have received a fraction of the attention, response times would have slipped, and the close rate would have fallen across the board — including on the good leads.

Qualification is not about rejecting people. It is about giving the right people enough attention to convert.

## What not to do

**Do not qualify on aggression.** Hard-selling everyone to see who survives filters for tolerance, not intent.

**Do not add fields that do not sort.** Company size on a B2C form is friction with no payoff.

**Do not discard the unqualified.** They are future customers with a different timeline. Nurture is cheap.

**Do not qualify only once.** Intent changes. Re-score on behaviour.

## The measurement that matters

Stop reporting cost per lead. Report **cost per qualified lead**, and eventually **cost per customer**.

A campaign producing ₹40 leads that never answer is more expensive than one producing ₹300 leads that close. Until your reporting reflects that, you will keep optimising toward the cheaper number and wondering why revenue does not follow.
