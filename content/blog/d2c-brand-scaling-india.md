Most Indian D2C brands do not fail at getting sales. They fail at getting sales that make money.

Revenue is easy to buy. You can hit ₹10L a month tomorrow by spending ₹9L on ads. The question is what remains.

## Start with contribution margin, not ROAS

Before touching an ad account, calculate what a sale is actually worth after product cost, shipping, packaging, payment gateway charges, and the cost of returns.

In Indian D2C, returns and RTO on cash-on-delivery orders are the silent killer. A brand with a 4X ROAS and 30% RTO is often less profitable than one at 2.8X with prepaid-only orders.

Your **breakeven ROAS** is what matters. If contribution margin is 40%, you need 2.5X just to stand still. Every campaign decision measures against that number, not against a benchmark from someone else's blog.

## Phase one: prove one product to one audience

Do not launch a catalogue campaign for eleven SKUs on day one.

Pick the product with the best margin and the clearest story. Run it to two or three distinct audience hypotheses with three creatives each. You are not looking for scale yet — you are looking for the combination where cost per purchase sits below breakeven consistently for two weeks.

Most brands skip this and scale a product that was never profitable, then blame the platform.

## Phase two: creative volume becomes the constraint

Once product-audience fit is proven, the bottleneck moves. It stops being targeting and becomes how many usable creatives you can produce per week.

Meta's algorithm finds the audience. Your job is giving it enough distinct angles to find them with: the problem angle, the transformation angle, the ingredient or craft angle, the social proof angle, the objection-handling angle.

Brands that scale past ₹10L monthly almost universally have a creative production system — not an agency that makes four ads a month.

## Phase three: fix the tracking before increasing budget

Scaling amplifies whatever signal you send. If your Pixel is missing a meaningful share of purchases because of browser restrictions, you are teaching the algorithm to find the wrong customers faster.

Server-side Conversion API with purchase value passed correctly is not optional at scale. On accounts where we have added it, the improvement in cost per result has repeatedly come more from data quality than from creative.

## Phase four: retention, or the maths stops working

Acquisition costs rise as you scale. That is not a failure — it is the auction. The brands that survive it are the ones where a customer is worth more than one order.

Two levers matter most in Indian D2C:

- **The second order.** A post-purchase WhatsApp flow that lands at the right interval, with a reason to reorder, changes lifetime value more than any ad optimisation.
- **Prepaid conversion.** Nudging COD customers to prepaid with a small incentive reduces RTO and improves cash flow simultaneously.

If lifetime value is 2.2X first order value, you can afford an acquisition cost that would bankrupt a competitor. That is the real scaling advantage.

## The mistakes that kill profitable brands

- **Doubling budget on a winner.** Sharp increases reset learning and push delivery into weaker inventory. Increase in steps, or use CBO and let allocation happen automatically.
- **Judging by ROAS on the dashboard.** Platform-reported ROAS and bank-account ROAS diverge. Reconcile monthly against actual revenue.
- **Discounting to hit a number.** Every discount comes directly out of contribution margin. A 20% off code on a 40% margin product halves your economics.
- **Ignoring frequency.** Above roughly 3.5, you are paying to irritate the same people. Costs climb and it looks like the campaign broke.

## A realistic timeline

Phase one takes four to six weeks. Phase two takes another two months to build a creative rhythm. Reaching consistent, profitable ₹10L months typically takes four to six months of disciplined work.

Anyone promising it in thirty days is either lucky or selling.
