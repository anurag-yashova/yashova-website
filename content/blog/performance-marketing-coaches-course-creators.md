Coaching and courses are trust purchases with a high refund-anxiety component. The buyer is not asking "is this good." They are asking "will I actually be able to do this, and will it change anything."

Advertising cannot answer either question. That is why direct-sale campaigns to cold audiences fail in this category.

## The entry offer is the whole strategy

The offer to cold traffic should never be the course. It should be a free experience with the person who teaches it — a live session, a workshop, a masterclass.

This reframes the decision entirely. Instead of evaluating a purchase, the prospect is evaluating a teacher. Most credible teachers pass that evaluation, and the sale becomes possible.

On a certification programme run this way, a free live webinar with the instructor produced 15,000 leads at ₹126 each, of which 780 enrolled — ₹1.02Cr revenue at 5.5X ROAS.

## Creative that works for this audience

Lead with the emotional state, not the curriculum.

Career-change buyers are usually anxious about being too late, too old, or from the wrong background. Creative that names those fears directly — *Career restart? It's never too late. No age barrier* — outperforms feature lists consistently.

Put the instructor's face and credentials in the creative. In a trust purchase, the person is the product.

## The registration-to-attendance gap

This is where most coaching funnels leak, and almost nobody measures it.

People register with genuine intent and then forget. Automated WhatsApp confirmation on registration — naming the session, the time, the instructor, and asking for a reply — does more for attendance than any creative change.

## Qualification before the sales call

Four questions inside the lead form:

- Have you attended a session before?
- What is your background?
- How soon do you want to start?
- Preferred language?

The timeline question sorts your entire pipeline. "Right away" and "just exploring" should never receive the same treatment, and small coaching teams cannot afford to discover the difference on a call.

## Pricing and the discount trap

Batch-based deadlines work in this category because they are real: the batch does start, and seats are finite.

Standing discounts do not work. If the price is always ₹11,999 down from ₹14,999, the anchor stops functioning and you have simply lowered your price permanently.

## Retargeting is where the revenue closes

Attendees who did not buy are the warmest audience you will ever have. Direct conversion campaigns belong here — never to cold traffic.

Carry the batch deadline, the specific outcome, and social proof from people in the same situation.

## The metrics to run this on

- Cost per registration
- Registration-to-attendance rate
- Attendance-to-enrolment rate
- Cost per enrolment
- Revenue per lead

Cost per lead alone will mislead you badly here, because a cheap lead who never attends is worth nothing and an expensive lead who enrols pays for twenty others.

## What to build first

1. One specific free session with a real date
2. Native lead form registration
3. Automated WhatsApp confirmation
4. Four qualification questions
5. Retargeting campaign for non-buyers

That is the entire machine. Everything more sophisticated is an optimisation of it.
