Most Indian institutions still run admissions on agents, hoardings and reputation. Those work, but they are not measurable and they do not scale on demand.

Paid acquisition adds a channel you can turn up in a weak year.

## The three revenue opportunities

**1. Core admissions.** The obvious one, and the hardest — highly seasonal, high competition, long consideration.

**2. Certification and skilling programmes.** Add-on courses sold to your existing students and to the open market. Better margins, year-round, far less competitive.

**3. Placement marketing.** Marketing your placement cell to recruiters rather than students. Improves outcomes, which improves admissions the following year.

Most institutions only attempt the first, which is the most expensive of the three.

## Admissions has a calendar, and it is unforgiving

The mistake is starting campaigns when applications open. By then the consideration process has already happened.

A workable shape:

- **Awareness phase**, well before applications — content, campus, faculty, outcomes
- **Consideration phase** — open days, webinars, counselling sessions
- **Application phase** — direct campaigns to the audience already engaged
- **Deadline phase** — retargeting only, with real deadlines

Cold direct-application campaigns in the final weeks are expensive and convert poorly.

## What parents respond to versus what students respond to

Both are decision-makers and they weigh different things.

Students respond to campus life, peer outcomes, and identity. Parents respond to placement records, fee structure, safety, and faculty credentials.

Run separate creative for both. A single ad trying to satisfy both usually satisfies neither.

## Certification programmes are the underused opportunity

They sell year-round, carry better margins, and face far less competition than degree admissions.

The funnel that works is the education-webinar structure: a free live session with the instructor, automated WhatsApp confirmation, qualification questions, and retargeting for non-buyers.

Run properly, this produced ₹1.02Cr in revenue on ₹18.6L of spend for a certification programme — 15,000 leads at ₹126, 780 admissions, 5.5X ROAS.

## Placement marketing to recruiters

Small budgets, precise targeting, disproportionate returns.

LinkedIn campaigns aimed at hiring managers in relevant industries, showing your students' skills and your training standards, generate recruiter interest that improves placement rates — which is the statistic that sells next year's admissions.

Educational content performs particularly well here. On a career-tech account, LinkedIn sponsored content led with hiring truths rather than product claims and held CPC at ₹4.69 across 386,438 impressions.

## Tracking, for institutions specifically

Applications and enquiries are not the same thing, and enrolment is neither. Define conversion events for each stage and send the enrolment data back so the platforms learn which enquiries actually became students.

Without that loop, you optimise for form fills during a season where form fills are cheap and enrolments are not.

## Where to start

If you have never run paid acquisition, do not start with core admissions. Start with a certification programme: shorter cycle, faster feedback, lower stakes, and it teaches your team the system before the season that matters.
