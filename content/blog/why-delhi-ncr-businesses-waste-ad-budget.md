"Waste" here does not mean fraud. It means spend that produced no possibility of revenue — clicks that could never convert, leads that were never contacted, conversions the platform never learned about.

These are the six leaks we find most often, roughly in order of how much they cost.

## 1. Incomplete conversion tracking

This is the largest and least visible leak.

If you run only the browser-side Meta Pixel, a substantial share of conversions never gets reported — iOS privacy restrictions and ad blockers see to that. The algorithm then optimises on a partial picture, learning to find people who look like your *measured* converters rather than your actual ones.

Every downstream decision inherits the error. Budget goes to the wrong campaign, creative is judged on wrong data, and audiences are built from an incomplete seed.

On an NGO account where we added server-side Conversion API alongside the Pixel, cost per result dropped from ₹175.61 to ₹85.48. The creative improved somewhat. The data improved far more.

**Fix:** implement Conversion API with events mapped to every meaningful action.

## 2. Traffic sent to a page that cannot convert

A landing page built like a brochure assumes the visitor already trusts you. Paid traffic does not.

If your page takes six seconds to load on a mid-range Android phone over 4G, a large share of visitors leave before seeing anything. You paid for every one of those clicks.

**Fix:** measure real mobile load time, cut the form to the fields you genuinely need, and put the offer above the fold. Then measure again.

## 3. Leads that nobody contacts fast enough

This one is entirely self-inflicted and extremely common.

A lead submitted at 3pm and called at 11am the next day is a different lead. Interest decays quickly, competitors respond faster, and the person has often already spoken to someone else.

**Fix:** automatic WhatsApp confirmation on submission, then a structured human follow-up sequence over the following week. The automation costs nothing per lead and captures attention at peak interest.

## 4. Scaling by doubling budget

When something works, the instinct is to double the budget. The result is usually that performance collapses.

Sharp budget increases reset the learning phase, push delivery into less qualified inventory, and drive frequency up until fatigue sets in. Between 2 and 3 frequency is generally healthy; above 3.5, you are paying to annoy the same people.

**Fix:** increase in steps, or use campaign budget optimisation and let the platform allocate. On one account, a single ad absorbed roughly 90% of the budget automatically because it genuinely outperformed the alternatives.

## 5. Optimising for the wrong event

If you tell Meta to optimise for link clicks, it will find people who click and never buy. It is doing exactly what you asked.

Optimise for the deepest event you have enough volume to support — purchase or qualified lead where possible, an intermediate event only when volume is too thin for the platform to learn.

**Fix:** audit what your campaigns are actually optimising for. It is frequently not what you assumed.

## 6. Judging leads on cost instead of quality

A ₹40 lead that never answers is more expensive than a ₹300 lead that closes. Cost per lead is a vanity metric when it is disconnected from what happens next.

**Fix:** track cost per *qualified* lead, and eventually cost per customer. Add qualification questions to the form so the sorting happens before your sales time is spent. On one education account, four form questions turned 15,000 raw leads into a workable list of 5,800 qualified prospects.

## How to find your own leaks in an hour

Work through this in order:

1. Check whether Conversion API is active, not just the Pixel.
2. Load your landing page on mobile data and time it.
3. Find out how long your last ten leads waited for first contact.
4. Look at what event each campaign is optimising for.
5. Check frequency across your top three ad sets.
6. Calculate cost per closed customer, not cost per lead.

Most businesses find at least three of the six. Each is fixable without spending more.

The reason these persist is that none of them show up as an obvious failure. The campaign runs, the dashboard shows numbers, the reports arrive. The money leaves quietly.
