Email open rates in India are poor. WhatsApp is where the conversation actually happens, and the businesses that automate it well convert leads that everyone else loses.

## Why speed decides outcomes

A lead submitted at 3pm and contacted at 11am the next day is not the same lead. In the intervening hours, interest cooled, a competitor replied, and the person moved on.

Automation solves the part humans cannot: responding within seconds, every time, at any hour, at zero marginal cost.

## Message 1: Instant confirmation

Fires on form submission. Confirms what they signed up for, when it happens, who they will deal with, and what comes next.

A structure that worked on an education account:

> Your webinar seat is confirmed. You're all set for the Medical Coding Live Webinar with Aparajita Sudarshan (18+ years experience). Sunday, 11th Jan, 3:00 PM. Your slot is locked for this live session. Joining link will be shared before the session. Please reply to confirm your availability.

Three things it does well: names a credible person, states specifics, and asks for a reply — which opens the 24-hour service window and signals intent.

## Message 2: Qualification

If you did not qualify inside the lead form, do it here. Four questions is the practical maximum:

1. What are you looking for specifically?
2. What is your timeline?
3. What is your budget range?
4. Preferred language?

Each answer sorts the lead. "Right away" and "just exploring" should enter completely different sequences.

## Message 3–6: The follow-up sequence

Most conversions in this market happen on the second or third touch. Most businesses stop after the first.

- **Day 0:** confirmation (automated)
- **Day 0 + 2 hours:** human message, personalised
- **Day 1:** value — a resource, a case study, an answer to the objection they raised
- **Day 3:** social proof — a result from a similar customer
- **Day 7:** direct ask with a deadline
- **Day 14:** soft close, move to long-term nurture

The value messages matter. A sequence of six "just following up" messages trains people to ignore you.

## Setup options

**WhatsApp Business App** — free, manual, fine under about 30 leads a month. No true automation.

**WhatsApp Business API via a provider** — the real answer for volume. Template messages, automated triggers, CRM integration. Costs are per-conversation and modest.

**Automation platform in between** — connects your lead form to the API, handles sequencing and branching without custom development.

## Template message rules

Meta must approve templates for messages sent outside the 24-hour window. Approval is straightforward if you follow the rules: be specific about who you are, do not be promotional in a utility template, and do not message people who did not opt in.

Once someone replies, you have a 24-hour window for free-form conversation. This is why the confirmation message ends with a question — it opens the window.

## What to measure

- **Delivery rate** — should be near total
- **Reply rate to message 1** — 30%+ is healthy
- **Lead-to-conversation rate**
- **Conversation-to-customer rate**

If delivery is high and replies are low, the message reads like marketing. Rewrite it as a confirmation, not a pitch.

## The mistake to avoid

Do not automate the entire relationship. Automation exists to make sure nobody falls through the gap between interest and contact, and to sort leads before human time is spent.

The conversation that closes the sale should be a human one. On the education account, automation handled confirmation and qualification across 15,000 leads; humans handled the 5,800 that mattered, and 780 enrolled.

That division of labour is the whole system.
