Most CRO advice is written for desktop traffic in wealthy markets. Indian paid traffic behaves differently, and the differences change what you should build.

## Assume mobile, mid-range, on data

Not a flagship phone on wifi. A mid-range Android on 4G, possibly with three other apps open.

Every second of load time costs conversions, and heavy hero images, autoplay video and multiple font files are where those seconds go. Test on the device your customers actually hold.

## The sequence that converts

The instinct is to lead with information. The correct order is:

1. **Credibility signal** — why should I believe you at all
2. **Specific outcome** — what exactly happens if I do this
3. **Frictionless ask** — the smallest possible next step

An informational page assumes the visitor already trusts you. Paid traffic does not. Trust must be built on the page, before the ask.

## Form length is a conversion lever, both ways

Fewer fields means more submissions. More fields means better leads.

The resolution is to ask qualifying questions rather than administrative ones. Name, phone, and two questions about intent beats name, email, phone, city, company, designation and a message box.

On one education funnel, four qualification questions — attendance, language, background, timeline — cut raw volume and produced a list the sales team could actually work: 5,800 qualified from 15,000 total, converting to 780 admissions.

## UPI first, always

On one donation campaign, **96.99% of payments came through UPI**. Cards were 3%.

If your checkout presents cards first and UPI as an option, you have inverted your own funnel. Put UPI at the top, make it one tap, and do not require app-switching more than once.

## Trust signals that work in India

- Real photographs of real people, not stock
- A phone number, visibly answerable
- A physical address
- Specific numbers rather than adjectives — "11,246 payments" beats "thousands of happy customers"
- Payment gateway logos at checkout
- WhatsApp as a contact option, because that is where people prefer to talk

## Copy: specificity beats sentiment

Every time. "Best coaching in Delhi" is noise. "Free 90-minute demo class Sunday, 12 seats, syllabus PDF included" is an offer.

Generic claims create mild positive feeling and no action. Specific claims create the sense that something identifiable happens if you click.

## What to test, in order

1. **The offer.** Biggest lever by far, and the one people skip because changing the offer feels harder than changing a button.
2. **The headline.** Does it name the visitor's actual problem?
3. **Form length.**
4. **Above-the-fold layout.** Is the ask visible without scrolling?
5. **Trust placement.** Move proof above the form.
6. **Button copy.** Smallest lever. Test it last, not first.

## Measure the right thing

Not bounce rate. Track:

- **Load time on mobile data** — real device, real network
- **Form start rate** — how many begin filling
- **Form completion rate** — how many finish
- **Qualified rate** — how many are worth calling

The gap between form start and form completion tells you the form is too long. The gap between visit and form start tells you the offer or headline is not landing.
